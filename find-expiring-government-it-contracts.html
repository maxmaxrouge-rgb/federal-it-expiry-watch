How to find Australian government IT contracts expiring in the next 6 months

Inferred: Use an expiry watchlist to decide which public records need further research before bid planning.

Inferred: This guide targets the query “how to find Australian government IT contracts expiring in the next 6 months”. The query reflects a supplier's planning task. Search volume and buyer demand are unverified.

1. Start with a dated issue

Open the 9 November 2026 sample issue. Record its date before using its entries.

2. Filter the CSV by date and service

Download the CSV. Open it in a spreadsheet. Filter “End date UTC” from 2026-11-09 through 2027-05-09, inclusive.

Filter “Description” for services or products your business can supply. Retain CN, agency, incumbent supplier and source URL.

Verified (local run): All 3 published CSV rows fall within that example date window.

Verified (fetched URL): The CSV records CN4045733, “Provision of End User Computing Hardware”, for Department of Agriculture, Fisheries and Forestry. It lists DELL AUSTRALIA PTY LTD and an end date of 2027-03-30.

Prior: These values come from the dated sample. They do not confirm a current tender or a future purchase.

3. Check the original record before acting

Open the row's AusTender source record. Compare the latest amendment, end date, scope and status with your saved row.

Record the check date and any changes. Remove cancelled contracts from your active watchlist.

Inferred: An end date is a research signal. It does not establish a new tender, renewal budget or supplier eligibility.

Check current tender notices and participation requirements separately before making a bid decision.

4. Watch for published issues

Add the Atom feed URL to a feed reader. Open linked issues and compare their dated rows with your watchlist.

Verified (fetched URL): The published Atom feed links to the sample issue. Prior: The current tracker follows a fixed cohort. It does not discover all federal IT contracts.

Keep the issue date beside each imported row. Check source records again when an entry affects a bid decision.
